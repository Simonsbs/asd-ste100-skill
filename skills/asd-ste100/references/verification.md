# Verification workflow

Separate what you read from what you inferred. A useful review is traceable and
limited to the evidence actually available.

## Establish the evidence

Record the requested edition and document identity. For a local PDF, confirm the
cover, issue date, and relevant pages. A filename alone does not prove an edition.
Issue 9 has 434 PDF pages. Its stable version DOI is
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23666006. The concept DOI,
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23666005, is useful for discovering later records.

For a source-based audit, use both part 1 and part 2. Verify each disputed general
word against the entry's part of speech, intended meaning, and inflected forms.
A dictionary search result can be a nonapproved entry, an example, or an
alternative suggestion. Its presence is not an approval decision.

Project terminology requires evidence such as a drawing, equipment label,
glossary, or terminology database. Record which category applies and whether
the term is a technical noun or verb. Unknown, unapproved, and valid technical
terms are different findings. If the category or meaning is uncertain, keep it open.

## Review the text

Check technical fidelity separately from language: all quantities, limits, units,
conditions, temporal relations, prohibitions, identifiers, and risk statements must
survive. Do not approve a technically wrong sentence because its grammar is simple.

Inspect the rules applicable to each passage type. For mixed documents, use the
local passage type rather than treating every paragraph as a procedure. Read
section 8 before judging borderline word counts, including lists, quoted labels,
units, parentheses, identifiers, and proper nouns.

If useful, keep this small review ledger:

| Location | Finding | Evidence | Correction or next check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paragraph or step | Confirmed, suspected, or unresolved | Issue, rule/page or term record | Concrete action |

Only call a rule violation confirmed when the rule and the relevant context were
actually checked. Give a reason for a discretionary editorial improvement.

## Report the limits

- Without the requested standard: a principles-only draft with no full verification.
- With partial source access: the checks performed and the checks still open.
- With rule and vocabulary review: a source-based review for the stated passage
  and edition, with remaining technical or specialist review identified.

Do not issue "ASD certified," "officially approved," or an unsupported percentage
of compliance. Human technical responsibility remains necessary, particularly for
operational and safety content. The official tools policy and AI white paper are
linked in sources.md.
