# Writing guide

This is a short, original orientation to Issue 9, not the complete rules.
Read the applicable rule and its explanation in your copy before making a detailed
conformance finding. Rule and page locations are in issue9-locators.json.

## Rule orientation

Procedural sentences have a 20-word limit (5.1), while descriptions have a 25-word
limit (6.3). Apply the special counting rules in section 8. Instructions use the
imperative (5.3). Separate instructions unless actions must occur together (5.2).
Place a necessary condition before its command (5.4). Notes give information (5.5).

Prefer active constructions. The descriptive exception concerns an unknown agent
(3.6). Verb forms need dictionary verification (3.1-3.4). An `-ing` spelling is not
automatically an error: technical-noun uses and approved nonverb entries matter
(3.5 and part 2). Do not manufacture a universal suffix ban.

Review noun groups longer than three words with the technical-noun provisions
(2.1-2.2). Keep necessary articles and other words (4.2, 4.5). Descriptive paragraphs
have one topic and at most six sentences (6.5-6.6). Use section 7 for safety
instructions and section 8 for punctuation. Semicolons are excluded (8.1).

## Working with source information

Maintain a small working list of facts that must survive the rewrite. Use it to
check the final text, especially when a condition applies to several steps. A
shorter sentence is useful only if it preserves those relationships.

For an ambiguous source such as "Release the lock after the test if the light is
green," determine whether the green light is a prerequisite to release or a test
result. Do not choose an interpretation just to make the sentence simpler.

When a source explicitly requires two actions at the same time, preserve that
timing. Replacing the actions with consecutive numbered steps can change the task.
When the source specifies a numbered label, retain the exact label and its mapping.

For warnings, keep the supplied risk, severity, condition, and avoidance action.
If any of those facts are absent, flag the gap. Do not invent a hazard explanation
to make a warning look complete. Respect controlled wording that requires a
separate technical or legal review and identify any unresolved language conflict.
